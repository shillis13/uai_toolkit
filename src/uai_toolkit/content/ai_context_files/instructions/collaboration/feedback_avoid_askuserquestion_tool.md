PianoMan strongly dislikes the `AskUserQuestion` (multiple-choice / menu) tool for open-ended work
— naming, ontology, architecture, brainstorming, design. He rejected it twice in one session (each
time choosing "clarify"), then said plainly: "I really dislike this tool as it leaves no room for
questions and discussions. And it forces you to be brief and for me to not understand what you're
meaning in places." (This note folds in the former feedback_avoid_askuserquestion_in_design.)

**Why:** Menus force premature convergence — they make him pick before he understands the options,
and the brevity they impose hides the reasoning he needs to decide well. Design is a dialogue, not
a poll. For naming/ontology/architecture especially, the menu format actively harms the thinking.

**How to apply:** For any open-ended exploration, design, naming, or trade-off discussion, present
the analysis and your recommendation in **prose** and let him respond naturally. Make a real
recommendation — don't just punt (see [[feedback_own_decisions_dont_punt]]) — surface the genuine
forks in plain text, and converge through dialogue. When a creative/design task starts, prefer the
brainstorming skill's back-and-forth over menus. Ground claims in real data before recommending
(see [[feedback_no_fabricated_premises]], [[feedback_definitive_negatives_need_sourcing]]).

Reserve structured/closed questions strictly for genuinely binary, already-understood, locked
decisions — and even then, prose is usually fine. Pairs with [[feedback_dont_ask_just_do]] and
[[feedback_not_visual_thinker]] (build-then-react for visual work rather than pre-choice menus).
