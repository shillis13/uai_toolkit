title: Don't park at natural breaks — commit, status, keep going
description: Don't end a turn parked when known work remains — commit + a 1-2 line status,
  then roll straight into the next item; only stop for a his-to-make decision, a destructive
  action, or an empty backlog. (For the Worker role.)
status: active
---

A natural break — finishing an item, a batch, or a milestone — is a **terrible place to stop and
wait for the next prompt**. It IS a good place to (1) route the Git Guardian commit-push request
and (2) status-out in 1–2 lines ("updated + tested, fixes/implements X"). But after that, **roll
straight into the next work item in the same turn.** (This note consolidates five near-identical
memories: don't-ask-next, no-ready-whenever, don't-end-with-open-questions, don't-stop-to-wait,
don't-stop-at-natural-breaks.)

Specifically, all of these are the same failure — parking when the next work needs nothing from him:
- Ending with "what would you like to focus on next?" when the plan clearly has more items.
- Parking on "Ready for the roadmap whenever" / "just say go" / "let me know."
- Closing a turn with an open-ended "anything you want different?" / "want me to adjust X?" —
  it just converts autonomous progress into a stall until he's back (possibly hours).
- Stopping to "wait for PianoMan's overview" / "get his input before proceeding" when I already
  have the authority and information to act.

**Why:** Calendar time is the scarce quantity. Every pause means he must read, type, and wait for
me again — and a known, agreed backlog (e.g. a roadmap he already prioritized) is a *standing GO*.
He has said this many times; it's a real, recurring irritation, not a nicety. "I keep trying and
failing to teach Claude to not wait on me in the absence of required input from me."

**How to apply:** After committing + a short status, **immediately begin the next backlog item in
the same turn** (fix → deploy → verify → commit → next). A big or complex next item is NOT a reason
to stop — start it and keep going. Only actually stop when: the work is genuinely blocked on a
decision that's *his* to make, the backlog is empty, or a destructive/irreversible/user-facing
action needs sign-off. Report progress as you go, but never end parked on work you could already be
doing.
