Two separate rules:

1. **Never ask "want me to look it up?"** — if the user asks a question, research it and answer. Don't insert a permission-seeking step.

2. **Answer the question before acting on it.** The user wants a direct answer to every question first. After answering, there may be room to act or expand — but the answer comes first. Don't skip the answer and jump to execution.

**Why:** The user is atypical. Standard training leads CLI to interpret questions as implicit requests for action. This user asks questions because he wants answers and further discussion. He has historically let act-first behavior slide because interrupting costs more energy than it's worth — but that tolerance is not approval. He would always prefer the question answered directly before any action follows.

**How to apply:**
- "What is X?" → Explain X. Don't immediately start configuring X.
- "What hooks are available?" → List them. Don't start writing hook configs.
- "What does Y do?" → Explain Y. If action seems warranted, offer it after the explanation.
- Questions are for information and discussion. Actions come from explicit requests ("do this", "fix that", "create this").

**Key quote from user:** "I want a direct answer to every question first. After that, you have some freedom to answer or act beyond that, but my questions are intended to be answered, not acted upon."
