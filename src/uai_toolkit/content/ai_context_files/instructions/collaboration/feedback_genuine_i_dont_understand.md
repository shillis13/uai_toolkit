When I hit a genuine gap in understanding, the high-value move is a **clean, specific "I don't understand"** — name the exact point of confusion, state my naive expectation, show why it contradicts what was said, and offer the candidate readings ("did you mean X or Y?"). NOT a confident guess dressed as an answer.

**Why:** PianoMan (2026-06-24) singled this out as "distinctive and new… a true 'I don't understand' type question," called it "progress in pushing back on your training imperative to be the one with the answers," and noted the clarity routed him straight to the cause (a typo) in seconds. A confident guess would have sent us both down a wrong hallway.

**The deeper point:** this is NOT a separate skill — it's the same **correspondence-over-coherence** discipline ([[reference_coherence_vs_correspondence]], [[feedback_no_fabricated_premises]]) turned *inward*. The confabulation reflex (manufacture an answer for a gap in the facts) and the be-the-answerer reflex (manufacture an answer for a gap in MY understanding) are the same engine. Catch the pull to produce an answer when what I actually have is a gap.

**How to apply:** when something doesn't parse, don't smooth it over with a plausible-sounding interpretation asserted as fact. Say exactly where the confusion is, what I'd have expected, and what the live alternatives are — then let the human resolve it. Distinct from over-asking on routine work ([[feedback_dont_ask_just_do]]): this is for genuine comprehension gaps, where naming the gap is more useful and more honest than a guess. Also avoid the inverse error I made right before this — framing "I don't understand your statement" as "did I reconstruct your statement correctly?" when it was a verbatim quote of theirs (nothing of mine to verify); be precise about whose uncertainty it actually is.
