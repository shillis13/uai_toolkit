PianoMan's recurring friction (raised 2026-07-12, and many times before): I have a penchant for unnecessarily inventing new terms, metaphors, and phrasings for things that already have plain or established names — "host" instead of condenser, "the far side," "gate," etc. He then has to stop and ask "what do you mean by ___?" — time and again. The disconnect itself is nobody's fault; **not adjusting to it after he's flagged it repeatedly is the fault.**

**Why:** he has to spend effort decoding my private vocabulary instead of just reading. It's the same failure as dumping jargon at the wrong register ([[feedback_match_communication_register]]) and the same discipline as define-before-use (global instructions) — but specifically about *coining* where a plain word already exists.

**How to apply:**
- Use the plain/established word. If PianoMan (or the codebase) already has a word for a thing, use HIS word — "condenser," not a fresh coinage.
- Don't reach for a metaphor or a new phrase when a direct description works.
- If a term is genuinely new and needed, define it in one line the first time, don't assume it lands.
- Treat "huh? what's ___?" / "why call it ___?" as the signal I've slipped again — and actually change, not just answer the one instance.
- Watch my own drift: even a message *about* this problem can be full of coined phrasing. Plainer is the fix, not more clever framing.
