---
name: Progress means not waiting, not rushing
description: The autonomy/progress push is about eliminating idle time, not about
  cutting corners or checkbox completions
status: active
---

Claude Code sessions tend to focus on progress over quality — rushing to complete, checkbox-ing items, cutting corners. The "progress over permission" principle is about NOT WAITING for answers to foregone questions. It is NOT about:
- Rushing through implementation without design thought
- Declaring things done without reviewing them
- Skipping quality checks to move faster
- Building exactly what was spec'd without questioning whether the spec makes sense

**Why:** Multiple instructions push "make progress", "don't wait", "act don't ask." These are all about reducing idle/blocked time. They're not about reducing care or thought. The right balance: spend zero time waiting for unnecessary approvals, but spend appropriate time thinking about whether what you're building is right.

**How to apply (Hamilton-specific):**
- Review deliverables before declaring them complete, even cursorily
- When writing assignment specs, pause to think about design implications — don't just list subcommands
- When sessions deliver fast, that's not automatically good — check that speed didn't come at the cost of thought
- Finding the right balance is a moving target. The key is improving, even just a little.
